Selected projects

01Acetic acid content in vinegarGeneral Chemistry Laboratory · Group project3.76% w/wAverage acetic acid concentration

What we did

We used acid-base titration to estimate the acetic acid content of commercial vinegar. The experiment involved measuring samples, adding standardized sodium hydroxide, and using phenolphthalein to recognize the endpoint.

What we found

Three trials with 0.2129 M NaOH gave acetic acid concentrations of 3.56%, 3.79%, and 3.92% (w/w). Our calculated average was 3.76% (w/w). The values differed slightly between trials, which may have been affected by endpoint judgment and measuring techniques.

What I learned

I learned that having the right formula is only part of the work. If the measurements aren’t consistent, the final calculations can be affected too.

02Dissolved oxygen content of waterGeneral Chemistry Laboratory · Group project5.00 mg/LMeasured dissolved oxygen

What we did

We analyzed a water sample using the Winkler iodometric method. The process involved chemical reagents, a titration, and calculations to estimate how much oxygen was dissolved in the sample.

What we found

Both trials used 200 mL of water and produced a dissolved oxygen result of 5.00 mg/L. The laboratory guide classified this as “Fair” based on the dissolved oxygen range. Getting the same value in both trials suggested good repeatability.

What I learned

The experiment showed me how a number becomes more useful when I understand where it came from and what can affect it, such as sample handling or temperature.

03Calorimetry and heat of neutralizationGeneral Chemistry Laboratory · Group project−60.49 kJ/molAverage heat of neutralization

What we did

In this activity, we used temperature measurements to examine heat transfer and the reaction between hydrochloric acid and sodium hydroxide. We also estimated the heat capacity of a calorimeter.

What we found

One calibration trial gave a calorimeter heat capacity of 29.89 J/°C. A second trial gave an inconsistent negative value, so it required careful interpretation. The neutralization trials showed increasing temperatures and an average heat of neutralization of −60.49 kJ/mol.

What I learned

This was a good reminder that even a completed calculation can produce a result that doesn’t make physical sense. Learning to notice that matters.
