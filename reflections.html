REFLECTIONS / JOURNAL

Notes from first year

A few short reflections on changing direction, doing laboratory work, and finding my footing in engineering.

01 / 04Changing programs

From Biology to AI Engineering

Studying Biology gave me a starting point, but over time I noticed that I preferred tasks involving numbers and logical reasoning. I kept coming back to the idea of studying engineering.

Moving into AI Engineering is a big change, and I’m still adjusting. But I’d rather take the time to learn something that genuinely interests me. Biology wasn’t wasted time—it taught me to observe carefully and ask questions.

02 / 04Working with numbers

Why the data matters

In chemistry laboratory, we didn’t just plug numbers into formulas. We had to collect measurements, calculate values, and explain the results.

The vinegar and dissolved-oxygen activities made me realize that a correct answer depends on reliable data. It’s a lesson I want to remember when I start working with datasets and models in AI Engineering.

03 / 04Mistakes and uncertainty

When the result looks wrong

Our calorimetry activity included one calculated negative heat capacity, even though that is not physically reasonable for a normal calorimeter. That made the limitations of a measurement very obvious.

I learned to ask what might have gone wrong, rather than just writing down a final value. In future engineering work, I want to be the kind of student who checks both the process and the result.

04 / 04First-year experience

Still at the beginning

AI Engineering as a Discipline has helped me think about the field beyond the fact that AI is popular right now. Engineering also involves responsibility, patience, communication, and being willing to keep learning.

Even simple activities—like learning about campus services or putting this portfolio together—have helped me become more organized. I have a lot to learn, and that’s exactly why I’m here.

I'll keep adding to this.
